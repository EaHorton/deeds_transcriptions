I saw this day at the Falls of Little River have the names of two negroes fixed on each
of three tickets of equal size on foolscap & had a person unconnected with
any concern with my decision to draw from the said Hat for my daughter
Mary Scyffor the wife of my son in law John Scyffor, two and Caroline
Scyffor, York, Polly, Nelson, for my daughter Amy Scyffor. Betsy & Thomas
Now Know all men by these Presents that I the said Joseph Gresham
in consideration of the natural love and affection which I have for and towards
my said Daughter Mary Scyffor & her husband John Scyffor & have given
and delivered over by these Presents a freely given grant and deliver to the said John and
Mary Scyffor the following slaves to wit, York, Polly, Nelson, for my daughter
Amy of one certain female slave named Lucy and in consideration of the
natural love and affection which I have for and towards my said daughter
Amy I do hereby freely give and grant and deliver to her and her husband
John Scyffor the said slaves York and Caroline. I then gave the said slaves
to my daughter Mary and Amy Scyffor to hold to them and their heirs forever against
the claim of myself my heirs assigns and all other persons. Nevertheless I am to retain
in the House with me two negroes if I continue to live in the District during
my natural life. Witness my hand and seal this Eleventh day of March
Anno Domini 1835
Signed sealed and delivered in presence of
Joseph Gresham (Seal)
Joseph Gresham
I was present and saw write the tickets & saw Hiram Reilly draw them out of the
Hat he knew nothing of what was intended, cannot read kept the Room, or even
of the names on the tickets, did not look appeared to be too far to
decide until it occurred to him that it was the equitable way to determine which of
the Negroes should belong to his respective Daughters. I had no motive or interest
in the matter further than to satisfy and oblige my old friend & Neigh. Witness
Joseph Gresham
State of South Carolina Personally came Joseph Gresham before me J. P.
Edmunds Quidam and made oath that he was present to keep the peace
and in the District aforesaid and made oath to due form of law and was
with said Gresham & Hiram Reilly and saw them draw and deliver the foregoing
and that he was entirely disinterested except for the love and affection he professed
and at first one that Frederick H. Gresham was with him and in his presence
and authority assisted in the execution of the same. Sworn to and subscribed
this 11th day of March 1835. Joseph Gresham
J. P. Edmunds J. P.
Recorded the 1st day of August 1835 and examined by me
William L. Reids C. C. of R. M. C.
