Sworn to and subscribed the 25th day of Augt 1842
John Beason, C.C.P.
Recorded the 25th day of February 1843 and examined by
W. L. Keith C.C. & M.E.

State of South Carolina. Know all men by these presents, that I
W. L. Keith of Prince William and State aforesaid, for and in consideration
of the sum of two hundred and fifty four dollars having been paid
to me in hand paid by James Hansell of the same place, have granted,
bargained, sold, and released, and by these presents do grant, bargain and sell
said James Hansell all that plantation or tract of land, situate, lying and being
situate in Prince William and State aforesaid on Cypress Creek on the East fork of Little
Salkehatchie River, adjoining lands of Elisha L. Huggins, James H. Huggins, and
late James H. Huggins, containing two hundred and fifty acres more or less, reference
being had to the original papers more fully described, and the said W. L. Keith
by these presents, for myself, my heirs, executors, and administrators, do warrant and
the right and title of the said plantation or tract of land, unto the said James Hansell,
his heirs and assigns forever, to have and to hold the said plantation or tract of land
forever with the said James Hansell his heirs and assigns forever, and I do hereby
bind myself, my heirs, executors, and administrators, to warrant and forever defend
the said plantation or tract of land, against myself and my heirs, and against
every other person or persons whomsoever lawfully claiming or to claim the same
unto the said James Hansell his heirs and assigns forever.

State of South Carolina. Before me personally appeared John Beason one of the within
witnesses, and made oath that he saw W. L. Keith sign and deliver the within deed of convey-
ance, as his act and deed for the uses and purposes within mentioned and E. H.
Lippincott with himself witnessed the same. Sworn to & subscribed this 25th day of Augt 1842
before me W. L. Keith C.C.
John Beason

Know all men by these presents, that I
John Prator of the County of Benton and
State of Alabama, for the consideration of the natural love and af-
fection I have for my daughter Margaret Hunter, late of
South Carolina & Robert Hunter and the further consideration of three hundred dollars
to me in hand paid, do give and grant unto the said Margaret Hunter and
to her bodily heirs forever, the named negroes, to wit, one negro girl named Rose and
increase, a negro boy named Sam, and a negro boy named Jim, to have and to hold the aforesaid
named negroes to the said Margaret Hunter and her bodily heirs forever, for the
benefit of the said Margaret Hunter bodily heirs forever, to wit, the children of said Margaret
Hunter, and I do warrant and defend the said gift, for the year of our Lord one thousand
eight hundred and forty two, this 21st day of January, in the year of our Lord one thousand
eight hundred and forty two.
John Prator

Before me personally appeared John Prator, who after being duly
sworn, saith that he saw the said John Prator sign, seal, and deliver the
within deed of gift to Margaret Hunter for the uses and purposes as therein expressed
and E. H. Lippincott with himself witnessed the same. Sworn to & subscribed this 21st day of
Jany 1842 before me John Prator
